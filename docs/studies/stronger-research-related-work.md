# Stronger research S0A: bounded primary-source related work

Reviewed October 23, 2026. This is a focused scoping review, not a systematic review or a priority/novelty claim. No empirical Anytime results were generated for it. Sources were located using NCM partial-reconstruction, adaptive sequence-generation/early-exit, ACT/PonderNet and optimal-stopping queries; primary arXiv/ACL papers and their relevant sections were checked. Secondary search results were used only to locate primary sources. Coverage includes older foundations and a recent inference-stopping example; it cannot certify that every relevant 2026 paper has been found.

## Partial reconstruction and the NCM hypothesis

**Hermann Kumbong et al. (2026), Next-Concept Modeling: Fast, Parallel Coarse-to-Fine Text Generation, arXiv:2610.14162v1.** [Primary paper, §3.3–3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14162v1#S3.SS3), [Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14162v1#A7), [pinned released source](https://github.com/HazyResearch/ncm/tree/c70274273f489abc73832b2b98584a8483b6b203). Multiscale residual quantization defines full-resolution cumulative reconstructions. Nested dropout evaluates tokenizer losses at sampled prefix cutoffs, encouraging useful partial information. Appendix G examines OWT target reconstruction and tokenizer failure cases; it is not an adaptive Sudoku/WMT stopping evaluation. The paper also evaluates generation, conditional tasks and reasoning. Inference: informative partial codes need not yield a complete exact Sudoku solution or a translation meeting corpus tolerances. Neither monotone per-input quality nor free intermediate decoding follows from the objective. The claim that NCM lacks any partial-reconstruction training would be incorrect; whether its particular objective/decoder limits the tested prefixes remains a hypothesis.

## Adaptive generative inference

**Maha Elbayad, Jiatao Gu, Edouard Grave and Michael Auli (2020), Depth-Adaptive Transformer, arXiv:1910.10073v4.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.10073v4), especially model/training and experiments. Trains sequence-to-sequence models to predict from intermediate decoder layers and studies sequence/token depth decisions on IWSLT German–English translation. Its adaptation skips Transformer layers, rather than NCM hierarchy levels followed by a separate full-resolution decoder. It motivates strong fixed-depth controls and separate treatment of output availability and exit prediction. It does not establish that a frozen released NCM decoder already exposes suitable early outputs, nor that layer counts transfer to NCM latency.

**Tal Schuster et al. (2022), Confident Adaptive Language Modeling (CALM), arXiv:2207.07061v2 / NeurIPS 2022.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.07061v2), §3–6 and Appendix C. CALM combines intermediate-layer prediction training, local confidence measures and calibration of global textual/risk consistency through Learn then Test; it handles missing earlier-token hidden states. Experiments use T5 on CNN/DM, WMT15 English–French and SQuAD, reporting task metrics and efficiency. Its BLEU reporting differs from calibration risk (BLEURT for translation). A local oracle agreeing with the final token is not our mixed-assignment corpus oracle. Takeaway: output sufficiency, causal confidence and sequence-level quality control are separate problems. CALM's guarantees and reported speedups cannot be transferred to this small reused WMT14 sample, its corpus BLEU constraints or a frozen NCM without the requisite assumptions, calibration and execution study.

**Anastasios N. Angelopoulos et al. (2021/2022), Learn then Test: Calibrating Predictive Algorithms to Achieve Risk Control, arXiv:2110.01052v5.** [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052v5). Proposes finite-sample risk calibration via hypothesis testing, illustrated across predictive tasks. It is a methodological basis for CALM, not a demonstration of adaptive NCM generation. Merely checking empirical tolerances after a search does not inherit distribution-free risk control; S1 will report conditional descriptive uncertainty and selection limitations instead.

## Learned halting and continuation objectives

**Alex Graves (2016/2017), Adaptive Computation Time for Recurrent Neural Networks, arXiv:1603.08983v6.** [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08983v6). ACT learns recurrent computational depth with a differentiable halting construction and computation penalty. Evaluations include synthetic parity/logic/addition/sorting and character-level Wikipedia language modeling. It supplies a training-based accuracy–computation objective, not a proof that current-validity classification equals continuation value or that saved generator-level counts imply latency.

**Andrea Banino, Jan Balaguer and Charles Blundell (2021), PonderNet: Learning to Ponder, arXiv:2107.05407v2.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.05407v2), §2–3. Learns a probabilistic halting distribution with expected prediction loss and KL regularization toward a geometric prior, encouraging exploration of computation lengths. Evaluations include parity, bAbI and reasoning tasks. Both the recurrence and halting behavior are trained; this is a different model scope from choosing exits of a frozen NCM. It motivates a cost-aware objective but is not a fitted continuation-value baseline for this project.

**Sebastian Becker, Patrick Cheridito and Arnulf Jentzen, Deep optimal stopping, arXiv:1804.05394v4 (2020 revision).** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.05394v4), §1–3. Studies learned stopping rules for simulated stochastic processes, with separate value bounds, and financial/fractional-Brownian examples. The decision compares immediate reward with conditional continuation value. A feasible rule and an upper certificate are distinct objects. Its Markov/simulation/reward setting and dual bounds do not automatically apply to nonlinear corpus BLEU constraints or correlated saved NCM prefixes.

**Yusuf Kalayci, Vinod Raman and Shaddin Dughmi (2025), Optimal Stopping vs Best-of-N for Inference Time Optimization, arXiv:2510.01394v1.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01394v1), §2–4 and experiments. Frames additional candidate generations as costly boxes and learns stopping thresholds, with AlpacaFarm/HH-RLHF reward-model evaluations. Candidate rewards and box assumptions differ from revising one NCM output; earlier hypotheses need not improve monotonically or remain selectable online. It is evidence for cost-aware adaptive-inference formulations, not transferable guarantees or a practical NCM baseline.

For a future study, a conceptual causal target is `E[best attainable future utility minus current utility minus additional execution cost | actually observed history]`, with terminal value zero and mandatory stop at maximum depth. This is our formulation, not an already trained model or a direct quotation. A scalar utility and its connection to corpus constraints would need a separate S3 protocol. Current validity estimates whether an answer is acceptable now; eventual-failure prediction estimates whether final output fails. Neither identifies expected marginal benefit without additional assumptions and labels. S1's reference/future-informed assignments do not estimate a deployable conditional expectation.

## Honest bounded negative evidence and evaluation

**Jesse Dodge et al. (2019), Show Your Work: Improved Reporting of Experimental Results.** [Primary paper](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1224/). Studies how computational search budgets and validation reporting change model comparisons. We adopt its motivation to disclose attempted candidates and budgets; we do not implement its expected-best-performance estimator or claim it proves our negative result. Failed searches, caps and missing measurements must remain visible.

**Philipp Koehn (2004), Statistical Significance Tests for Machine Translation Evaluation.** [Primary paper](https://aclanthology.org/W04-3250/). Studies bootstrap significance testing for MT/BLEU. It motivates paired corpus resampling, not averaging sentence BLEU. Resampling previously optimized assignments while keeping exits fixed gives conditional uncertainty; it neither accounts fully for search selection nor makes reused development data fresh confirmation.

## Contribution boundary and unresolved alternatives

The defensible prospective contribution is an evidence-ranked separation of (1) usable answers present in saved prefixes, (2) ability of causal policies to recognize them, and (3) sufficient saved execution cost to overcome monitoring. It is not yet a new stopping algorithm or a novelty claim. The [inventory](stronger-research-inventory.md) links the established negative observations.

Competing explanations remain: target representation/decoder behavior, generated-code errors, sensitivity to missing residual levels, weak stopping signals and restrictive metrics/measurement. NCM's partial training supplies counterweight to a simplistic explanation based on no intermediate supervision; R1B narrows a generator-only explanation without isolating a mechanism. A saved WMT assignment search may distinguish a missed opportunity from no opportunity found within budget. It cannot prove a bottleneck, establish semantic safety, or justify changing tasks/checkpoints until something succeeds. A negative or inconclusive finite search is a valid result.
